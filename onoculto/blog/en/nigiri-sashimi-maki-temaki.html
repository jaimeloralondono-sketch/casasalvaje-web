Nigiri, sashimi, maki or temaki: how to order sushi without getting lost

A sushi menu has its own language. This short guide helps you read it, order well and eat each piece the right way.

By ONO Hosomaki Bar

The first time you open a sushi menu, the words can be intimidating. They needn't be: five names cover almost everything, and the rest you learn by tasting.

The five words that matter

Nigiri: a pillow of hand-shaped rice with a slice of fish on top. Sashimi: the fish alone, thinly cut, no rice. Maki: the roll, rice and filling rolled in nori; if it's thin with a single ingredient, it's a hosomaki. Uramaki: the inside-out roll, rice on the outside. Temaki or hand roll: a cone of nori, filled, eaten by hand and freshly made, before the seaweed loses its crunch.

Five words cover almost the whole menu.

How to eat it

Nigiri can be eaten by hand or with chopsticks, in one bite, and if you dip it in soy, dip the fish side, not the rice, so it doesn't fall apart. Pickled ginger cleans the palate between pieces. Wasabi goes in small amounts: the point is to accompany the fish, not bury it.

What to order the first time

Start simple: a hosomaki and a couple of nigiri of the same fish, to taste it two ways. Then a house roll, and a temaki to finish.
