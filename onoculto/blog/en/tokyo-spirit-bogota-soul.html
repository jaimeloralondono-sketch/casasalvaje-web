Tokyo spirit, Bogotá soul: the story behind ONO

A contemporary temple where Japan meets Bogotá's night. What ONO means, where the Oni mask comes from and why everything here has two faces.

By ONO Hosomaki Bar

Tokyo spirit, Bogotá soul. The line that sums up ONO fits in four words and two cities. On the Tokyo side, precision: the exact cut, rice at just the right temperature, the quiet of a well-run bar. On the Bogotá side, soul: long conversations, music that rises without asking, and a night that refuses to end.

The Oni: light and shadow

ONO is inspired by the Oni, creatures of Japanese folklore that stand for human duality: light and shadow, chaos and order. That's why the Oni mask is in everything we do. It isn't a costume: it's an invitation to show the face you keep for the night. Unmask your Oni.

ONO is where the seen and the hidden meet.

A temple with two lives

The room has two lives. At 7 pm it's a softly lit restaurant: shoji screens, dark timber, hosomaki and signature cocktails. At 11 pm the lights drop, the Funktion-One sound rises and the same room becomes a club. Nobody moves; it's the place that sheds its skin.

What matters to us

Precision, because every nigiri, every drink and every sound is made with intent. Hospitality, because whoever walks into ONO walks into a temple. Discretion, because ONO is a refuge and luxury is also silence. And beauty, because it guides everything, from plating to light.

Come and see it

ONO Hosomaki Bar is at Calle 84A #9-11, Bogotá, Tuesday to Saturday, 7 pm to 3 am, for guests 25 and over. If it's your first time, read our guide to sushi in Bogotá or what the party is like, and book your table.
